SUN LAB / OUR SCIENCE

Research

We develop and apply single-cell multi-omics technologies to understand cell states and gene regulation. Our research combines comparative biology, models of regulatory DNA, functional experiments and studies of immune recognition.

Single-cell multi-omics technology development

We develop new single-cell multi-omics sequencing methods, with a focus on scRNA-seq, scATAC-seq and single-cell CUT&Tag. These technologies provide the experimental basis for studying gene expression, chromatin accessibility and chromatin-associated features across cell states and biological contexts.

Comparative atlases across development and adulthood

We build single-cell multi-omics atlases of embryonic and adult tissues across species. By comparing cellular states and regulatory landscapes, we seek to identify conserved and species-specific mechanisms of gene regulation and understand how they have changed during evolution.

Chronic stress and epigenetic remodeling

We map how sustained physiological stresses, including obesity, hypertension and sleep deprivation, reshape cellular epigenetic states. We investigate whether and how these changes alter cell function and disease susceptibility, including possible links to tumor development.

Deep learning and functional enhancer genomics

We combine sequence-to-function modeling with ChromBPNet and CREsted, massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs), and other functional genomics approaches. We aim to understand how enhancer activity depends on sequence and cellular context, and to test enhancer function across biological conditions.

MHC-I–peptide–TCR recognition

We study the interaction rules that govern peptide presentation by MHC class I (MHC-I) and recognition by T-cell receptors (TCRs). We aim to use these insights to reduce unwanted MHC-I-mediated immune responses to exogenous proteins used in therapy.
